CLICK ME

I’m near the end of a cycle; And lately I’ve been thinking about what’s coming next — that is still vague.

Three years have passed and I’ve grown. A lot;
But still, I don't feel neither understood, nor fully part of something; And I don’t think I’ll ever be.

However, I’ve been questioning everything, because it feels so weird to still want to be anything else in life, when we are facing an apocalyptic landscape where all the crisis are happening simultaneously. When everything seems to be falling apart, I just started to CARE LESS about my actions repercussions;

Which is good for someone who overthinks and also it’s so relieving to know that nobody really cares about
what we’re doing or if we’re good enough. I guess that’s one benefit of living in a nihilist era in the middle of a crisis: you start taking risks, as a stress response to the status-quo; And then some surprises may come along the way.

Everything feels suspended; That’s life at the cliff of an orgasm that never comes.

I stopped making big plans for the future and started living with the doubt. There’s a big desire to be independent, be seen and valued, as many of my friends also do. But at the same time, it hurts a lot when nothing hits my mail box. Or even when nothing seems to feel right at the moment.

I’ve been swinging between wanting stability and also wanting chaos in my life; In order to find something new, I truly believe that I can’t set myself too comfortably. There’s something interesting in inhabiting this tension; A sense of mystery and danger seems to unravel & Life feels interesting again; I never felt so complete in being attached and detached to everything.

My purest desire is not having to prove anything to anybody. Not needing someone to validate my worth.
Not needing an important job or role in life to validate what I have to say.
Not depending on external things to just simply exist.
And after all the noise, I wish to disappear.

And yet, the world is ending, however I still CARE; In this 3 years, I learnt that love is structural and political; And maybe that’s the only thing worth competing for.

As a communication designer, there are things that keep me putting on track: putting effort into what I believe is contemporary and relevant (or maybe not); choosing curiosity over fear, which can lead you to improbable futures; redirecting my energy from the ego to the collective. I’m not creating as an individual, but as part of something even culturally bigger, so it makes a lot of sense when creative work is shared.

At the end, I must confess that the communication design degree made me hotter. What’s next?
